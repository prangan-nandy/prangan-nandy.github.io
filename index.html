About

Research at the intersection of modeling and experiment.

I am a Graduate Research Assistant in the RASAER Laboratory at the University of South Carolina, where I investigate atmospheric-pressure plasma generation, plasma-surface interactions, and plasma-assisted chemical conversion.

My work connects numerical models with synchronized electrical diagnostics, ICCD imaging, optical emission spectroscopy, and purpose-built experimental systems. I enjoy translating complex physical behavior into models and measurements that reveal the mechanisms driving a system.

Previously, I developed engineering simulation tools, supported sustainable-product data systems, and improved industrial maintenance processes through root-cause analysis and mechanical design.

Currently based in Columbia, South Carolina

Selected research

Projects built around difficult coupled-physics questions.

From piezoelectric plasma sources to sustainable energy systems, these projects combine mechanistic modeling with practical engineering.

NSF ECLIPSE / 2024 - Present

Piezoelectric transformer-driven plasma sources

Developed a fully coupled 2D framework linking piezoelectric mechanics and plasma kinetics, then paired it with synchronized voltage-current diagnostics and time-resolved ICCD imaging to explain polarity-dependent surface-guided discharges.

U.S. Department of Energy / 2024 - 2025

Plasma-assisted siloxane reformation

Implemented a global plasma-chemistry model to interpret HMDSO conversion, fragmentation pathways, reactive intermediates, and power-dependent gas-phase clustering measured with molecular-beam mass spectrometry.

Plasma-enabled lignin hydrogenolysis
